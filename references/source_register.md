## Additional statistical context

[Cover (1973)](https://isl.stanford.edu/~cover/papers/paper26.pdf) studies eventual identification of a mean's membership in a countable set with exceptional parameters. [Dembo and Peres (1994)](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176325360) give topological criteria for statistical hypothesis testing. Cover's primary paper was inspected; the Dembo–Peres comparison is limited to its abstract and bibliographic record. Neither is invoked as a theorem implying the canonical-output result proved in this repository.

The later geometric-partition literature includes *Replicability in Learning: Geometric Partitions and KKM-Sperner Lemma*, NeurIPS 2024 ([primary PDF](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/8ff87c96935244b63503f542472462b3-Paper-Conference.pdf)). A recent followup is *The Price of List Replicability: Tight Bounds for Bias Estimation*, arXiv:2611.04532 ([primary identifier](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04532)); only its indexed abstract was available in this comparison. These sources concern list and sample complexity. No exact pointwise Baire theorem is attributed to them without a corresponding proof inspection.

## Reading boundary

The Baire argument, Gibbs-replacement channel, resource accounting, and output-entropy estimates are stated and proved in the scientific pages. The references supply imported ingredients and comparisons. The absence of a matching statement in this bounded source set does not establish that every component is new. In particular, standard projection-and-replacement channels, relative-entropy approximation, diagonal schedules, and two-list rounding are not claimed as new mathematical principles.
